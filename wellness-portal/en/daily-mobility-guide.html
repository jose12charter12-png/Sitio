Daily mobility: how to add movement breaks

Simple ideas to interrupt long periods of sitting and move more regularly.

Make movement part of your day

Everyday mobility does not need to be a long session or complicated routine. For many people, a practical first step is noticing long periods of inactivity and planning small opportunities to change position and walk.

Use realistic reminders

Connect a break to activities you already do: after a meeting, when finishing a task or while waiting for coffee. A recurring reminder can help build consistency.

Vary your positions

If you work seated, consider standing up, walking for a few minutes or doing gentle movements within a comfortable range. Changing positions is often more sustainable than trying to hold one perfect posture for hours.

Start with simple movements

You might walk around the room, gently move your shoulders, ankles and wrists or perform a few supported calf raises. Avoid abrupt bouncing and do not force joints to their limits.

Adapt breaks to your circumstances

Movement tolerance varies. If you have persistent pain, an injury, dizziness or mobility limitations, seek individual guidance before trying new exercises. A break should feel manageable, not like an endurance test.

Track your routine

For one week, notice when you spend the longest periods without moving and which breaks are easiest to repeat. Adjust frequency to your tasks and comfort.

Reference

World Health Organization: physical activity and sedentary behaviour.
